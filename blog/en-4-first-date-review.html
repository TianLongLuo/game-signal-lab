How to Review a First Date with Notes Instead of Intuition

2026-08-03 · GAME Signal Lab Blog · Chinese version

After a first date ends, your brain is running hot. Did they like me? Did I say something weird? What did that silence mean? In this state, your emotions are the least reliable navigator. The most reliable tool is a review note — written while the details are still fresh.

Write within 24 hours

Memory is malleable. What you remember three days after a first date often differs from what actually happened. Research shows that every time you recall a memory, you reconstruct it — and emotions accelerate that reconstruction. That's why you need to pin down the details while they're still accurate.

What to record

Write down: What topics did you talk about? Who changed the subject most often? What was their body language — eye contact, posture, hand gestures? Were there silences, and if so, who broke them first? Did they bring up future plans? Did they explicitly mention meeting again? These are facts. Don't add interpretation yet.

Then analyze

Below your facts, start a second section for interpretations. But here's the rule: every interpretation must come with a piece of evidence. If you wrote "they seemed interested," what's the evidence — they offered to pay? They mentioned a next meeting? They extended the date past the planned time? Writing out the evidence reveals how many interpretations have no legs under them.

When to revisit

Return to your notes three days later. If you had a second date, compare the two sets of notes. Did your first-date judgments hold up? Did details you noticed then prove meaningful later? This before-and-after comparison is the core power of review.
