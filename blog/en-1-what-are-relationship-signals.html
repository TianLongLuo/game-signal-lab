What Are Relationship Signals? How to Separate Facts from Guesses

2026-08-03 · GAME Signal Lab Blog · 中文版

Every day in a relationship, we're flooded with information. She hasn't texted you today — that's a fact. She hasn't texted you today, so she's losing interest — that might be a fact, or it might be a guess. Knowing which is which is the foundation of every honest relationship review.

What counts as a fact?

A fact is something verifiable. She replied to your message at 8pm on Tuesday. You had dinner together last week and he paid. She said three months ago that she "wasn't looking for anything serious right now." These things happened and don't depend on how you feel today. They can be corroborated.

What's a guess?

A guess is your interpretation of a fact. She hasn't replied in three days — that's a fact. She hasn't replied in three days, so she doesn't care about you — that's a guess. Maybe she's traveling. Maybe her phone broke. Maybe she needs alone time. You cannot know the real reason without more evidence.

Why does this distinction matter?

When you treat a guess as a fact, your actions drift off course. You start responding coldly because you've concluded "she doesn't care" — but she was simply on a work trip. Your coldness then becomes the actual damage in the relationship. So many misunderstandings begin with one person treating a guess like the truth and making decisions from there.

A simple training exercise

Take a piece of paper and draw two columns. Left column: "What I observed as a fact." Right column: "My interpretations of that fact." For example, left: "He canceled tonight's date." Right: "He might be losing interest / He might have an emergency / He might have forgotten." When you list your interpretations, you'll notice that a single fact can generate multiple possible explanations — and your emotions usually amplify the worst one.

Facts keep you grounded. Guesses lead you astray. Step one of any relationship review is separating the two.
